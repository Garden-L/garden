# Specification

Treat a specification as a shared promise between the people who need a result and the people who will create and judge it. Preserve the intent and its source. Separate what is known, assumed, decided, and still open.

Make each promise clear enough to interpret consistently and concrete enough to evaluate. Derive observations from the promise itself: what conditions apply, what result should be visible, and what evidence would support a decision. Keep promises and observations connected in both directions.

Do not hide ambiguity with convenient wording or invent expected behavior. A domain's terminology, format, standards, and approval process shape the document, but do not replace the need for clear meaning and traceable evidence.

Each stated obligation or claim needs a way to decide whether it has been met or supported. Keep that decision condition close to the statement. When the method for making the decision is maintained separately, link it clearly and avoid duplicating details that can drift apart.

When an issue requires a choice whose effect spans artifacts, apply `decision` to assess the options and connect the authorized choice to its application and verification. Use `why` and `how` directly for explanations that do not require a continuing decision.
